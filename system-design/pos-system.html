Case Study 04

POS & Inventory System

A POS must keep checkout and stock writes correct while product browsing, reports and side effects scale independently. This case study shows how Laravel app servers, database servers, Redis and workers evolve from one user to a very large store network.

Why simple?

One cashier does not need a load balancer, Redis cluster or database replicas.

Why Redis?

Product/catalog reads and shared session data can come from memory, while authoritative inventory writes remain in MySQL.

Concurrency

Row locks or optimistic version checks protect the final stock units when two cashiers sell at the same time.

Workers

Reports, emails and large exports run in background workers rather than blocking cashier requests.

Checkout path

Sales and inventory writes still go to the transactional primary. Product browsing and safe reads can use cache/replicas.

Store identity

Every stock record needs store/location keys. Idempotency keys stop duplicate sale creation when terminals retry requests.

Reporting isolation

Operational checkout queries should not compete with large historical reports; replicas or a reporting store handle analytical reads.

Regional isolation

Large store networks can keep operational traffic close to a region and reduce the blast radius of a single infrastructure failure.

Offline stores

Where WAN connectivity is unreliable, local queued sales and controlled synchronization need explicit conflict/idempotency rules.

Events

Completed sales can publish events to analytics, loyalty, notifications and integrations without coupling those systems directly to checkout.

*App-server ranges are examples, not guarantees

A POS serving millions of customer accounts may still have far fewer concurrent cashiers. Size Laravel servers from peak checkouts per second, catalog traffic, PHP-FPM utilization and database transaction latency—not registered user count alone.
